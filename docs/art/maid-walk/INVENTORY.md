## Cadence and foot sliding (known limitation)

Every sequence is a slow walk in place: the planted foot travels 1-2.4 px per frame at 120 px character height, which is 0.33-0.75 world units per cycle. A maid at 1x walks 2.2+ units/s, so a cycle that never slid would have to play at 100+ drawings per second and strobe. The catalog therefore plays each cycle at ~30 drawings/s at 1x (`units_per_cycle = frames * 2.2 / 30`) and `WalkCycle` caps it at 60/s at 2x and 4x. The `slide_factor` column is how many times faster the maid travels than her feet would: the feet slide at every speed. This cannot be fixed without new footage with a faster stride; nothing artificial was added to hide it.

## Identity decisions

- An earlier `01_suzu/.../new_frames` export drew Hina's design and was rejected; the owner replaced the folder with the correct mint Suzu, now integrated.
- Hina's local frames are the Hina design: used for Hina.
- Runtime resolution is unchanged in spirit: **name and archetype must both match** (`MaidAnimCatalog.Resolve`). A drawn "Hina" of another archetype stays procedural. Nothing is persisted and no save migration exists; a persistent `visual_id` (or one design per archetype) is a product decision left to the owner.
- Any equipped gear returns the procedural layered figure, because gear is drawn as layers a flat sheet cannot show.

## Only walk exists

Idle reuses a both-feet-down frame; Carry shows the walk plus the existing plate; Cook reuses idle/walk; Tired dims the sprite. There are no authored Carry, Cook or Tired animations.

## Style trade-off

Since ART-04 frames are reduced once (Lanczos, premultiplied alpha) to 240 px character height with original colours and soft alpha (`style: illustrated`). The ART-03 treatment (120 px, 48-colour palette, hard alpha) is `--style pixel` / commit `37b0f3f`. Foot measurements are taken on a 120 px copy so rest frames, idle frame and `units_per_cycle` do not depend on texture sharpness.
